India's Tightrope Walk

A deep dive into how India navigates the complex world of international sanctions, balancing its alliances, national interests, and the pressures of a polarized global order.

The Enforcer

India rigorously follows all sanctions mandated by the United Nations Security Council, reflecting its commitment to international law.

The Resistor

India consistently rejects all unilateral sanctions imposed by individual countries, prioritizing its "strategic autonomy."

The Playbook

Understanding the 'why' behind India's actions through its historical policy and legal framework.

1947: Non-Alignment

After independence, India co-founds the Non-Aligned Movement, charting a neutral course during the Cold War.

1998: Sanctions Target

India faces unilateral sanctions after its nuclear tests, hardening its stance against such measures.

2010s: Multi-Alignment

India begins engaging with multiple major powers simultaneously, balancing relationships with the US, Russia, and others.

Today: Strategic Autonomy

India's modern doctrine: an issue-based policy that prioritizes national interest and flexibility above all.

The Legal Toolkit

UN (Security Council) Act, 1947

Grants government power to enforce any UN Security Council decision.

Foreign Trade Act, 1992

Provides the regulatory tools to prohibit or restrict trade with sanctioned nations.

IT Act, 2000 (Section 69A)

Empowers the government to block public access to online content for national security.
