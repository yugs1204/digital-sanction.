The Dual Faces of Sanctions in Indian Statecraft

India operates with a dual identity: a disciplined implementer of UN-mandated sanctions and a staunch opponent of unilateral coercive measures.

Enforcer of Multilateralism

India consistently implements sanctions mandated by the United Nations Security Council (UNSC), reflecting its commitment to the collective security system through domestic laws like The UN (Security Council) Act, 1947.

Guardian of Strategic Autonomy

Shaped by its own history as a sanctions target, India does not recognize unilateral sanctions. This doctrine prioritizes national interest, flexibility, and avoidance of binding alliances that constrain decision-making.

India as an Enforcer of Economic Coercion

While opposed to unilateralism, India has deployed targeted economic measures in response to direct threats to its sovereignty and security.

Case Study: Pakistan Post-Pulwama

Following the 2019 Pulwama attack, India revoked Pakistan's Most Favored Nation (MFN) status and imposed a prohibitive 200% customs duty. This asymmetric move effectively halted formal trade, with imports collapsing by 99.9% from $488.5M to just $0.42M, sending a powerful political signal at minimal economic cost to India.

Case Study: China Post-Galwan

In response to the 2020 border clashes, India initiated "digital strikes," banning hundreds of Chinese mobile apps. This action weaponized India's vast consumer market, erasing multi-billion dollar growth opportunities for firms like ByteDance (TikTok) and establishing a potent new non-military geopolitical tool.

India as a Subject of Sanctions Pressure

India has navigated the disruptive landscape of extraterritorial sanctions, with its response evolving from compliance to strategic defiance.

The Iran Dilemma: Reluctant Compliance

Faced with U.S. secondary sanctions after 2018, India was forced to wind down crude oil imports from Iran, a key energy partner. Imports dropped from over 450,000 barrels per day to zero by mid-2019, forcing a costly restructuring of India's energy supply chain.

The Russia Conundrum: Strategic Defiance

Following the 2022 Ukraine invasion, India adopted a defiant posture. Refusing to join Western sanctions, it capitalized on discounted Russian oil, saving an estimated $10 billion and becoming Russia's largest seaborne crude importer. This move, however, created friction with Western partners.

Conclusion & Strategic Recommendations

India's dual-track sanctions policy has been a pragmatic and largely effective balancing act, but long-term sustainability requires enhanced resilience and skilled diplomacy.

Strengthen Resilience

Diversify energy suppliers, accelerate renewables, and boost domestic defense production to reduce vulnerabilities.

Lead Global South

Use platforms like BRICS and G20 to build consensus against unilateral coercive economic measures.

Develop 'Proofing'

Invest in non-dollar payment systems and alternative trade corridors to create resilient supply chains.

Calibrated Diplomacy

Maintain open communication with Western partners to manage friction and highlight areas of strategic convergence.
